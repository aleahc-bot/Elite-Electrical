Frequently Asked Electrical Panel Services Questions

How do I know if I need an electrical panel upgrade?

You may need an upgrade if your home still uses fuses, breakers trip constantly, you notice flickering lights, or you are adding heavy electrical draws such as an EV charger, pool heater, or heat pump. In Southwest Florida, panels manufactured by Federal Pacific Electric (FPE), Zinsco, or Challenger should also be replaced immediately due to documented safety and fire hazards.

Why is my homeowner’s insurance requiring me to replace my electrical panel?

Many insurance carriers operating in Florida now refuse to issue or renew coverage on homes with obsolete panels—most notably Federal Pacific, Zinsco, and obsolete split-bus designs. Because these panels frequently fail to trip under overload conditions, insurers consider them unacceptable fire risks. Our state-certified team (License #EC13003980) replaces these panels to full Florida Building Code standards and provides the necessary completion paperwork for your insurance four-point inspection.

What is the difference between a panel upgrade and a heavy-up?

A standard panel upgrade replaces the enclosure, interior bus bars, and breakers with modern components while maintaining your current amperage capacity (e.g., swapping an old 150-amp box for a new 150-amp panel). A “heavy-up” increases the overall utility amperage entering your home—typically upgrading from 100 or 150 amps to 200 or 400 amps—which also requires upgrading the exterior meter can, weatherhead, and service entrance conductors.

Do you coordinate with the local utility company (FPL, LCEC) during a panel upgrade?

Yes. We pull the municipal electrical permit under Florida License #EC13003980, submit the service work order, and coordinate directly with Florida Power & Light (FPL) or Lee County Electric Cooperative (LCEC) to schedule the temporary power disconnect, exterior meter release, and same-day reconnection once the city or county rough inspection passes.
